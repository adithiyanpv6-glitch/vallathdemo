---
title: Alexander Pope and the Mock-Heroic Epic
author: Alexander Pope
period: Neoclassical / Augustan Age (18th Century)
topic: Satire and Form
---

Alexander Pope is the preeminent satirist of the English Augustan Age, perfecting the closed heroic couplet (rhymed iambic pentameter) with unmatched antithesis, balance, and metrical precision. In *The Rape of the Lock* (1712, expanded 1714), Pope utilized the mock-heroic or mock-epic form to satirize a real-world quarrel between two aristocratic Catholic families caused by Lord Petre snipping a lock of Arabella Fermor's hair. 

By applying the grand conventions of classical epics—invocations to the muse, divine machinery (sylphs and gnomes), epic battle descriptions, arming of the hero (Belinda's toilet table rituals), and descent into the underworld (the Cave of Spleen)—to a trivial domestic squabble, Pope satirized the vanity, moral superficiality, and distorted values of eighteenth-century London high society, where petticoats and bibles share the same cosmetic clutter.
