---
title: Structuralism: Saussure's Signifier and Signified
author: Ferdinand de Saussure
period: Twentieth Century Structuralism
topic: Linguistics and Theory
---

Structuralism analyzes cultural artifacts and literary texts as systems governed by underlying grammatical codes and structural relations. It originates in the posthumous Course in General Linguistics (1916) by Swiss linguist Ferdinand de Saussure. Saussure distinguished between langue (the abstract, shared system of rules and conventions of a language) and parole (individual spoken or written speech acts). 

Saussure established that the linguistic sign is bipartite, composed of the signifier (the sound-image or written word) and the signified (the mental concept). Crucially, the link between signifier and signified is arbitrary—there is no natural, necessary connection between the word 'tree' and the physical object. Language is a differential system without positive terms: signs possess meaning only through their differences and binary oppositions relative to other signs in the semiotic system.
