---
title: Russian Formalism: Defamiliarization & Fabula/Syuzhet
author: Viktor Shklovsky
period: Early Twentieth Century (1910s–1930s)
topic: Literary Theory
---

Russian Formalism, flourishing in Moscow and St. Petersburg through figures like Viktor Shklovsky, Boris Eikhenbaum, and Roman Jakobson, sought to establish a scientific study of literature based on 'literariness' (literaturnost)—the specific linguistic qualities that make a verbal work art. 

In his 1917 manifesto 'Art as Technique', Viktor Shklovsky formulated the concept of ostranenie (defamiliarization or 'making strange'). Shklovsky argued that habitual perception dulls our sensory experience of objects; the purpose of art is to disrupt automated perception by making forms difficult and prolonging the perceptual process. The Formalists also distinguished between fabula (the chronological raw story events in reality) and syuzhet (the artistic plot arrangement and stylistic deformation of those events by the author).
