---
title: Petrarchan vs Shakespearean Sonnets
author: Francesco Petrarch / William Shakespeare
period: Renaissance / Early Modern
topic: Poetic Forms
---

The sonnet entered English literature through Sir Thomas Wyatt and the Earl of Surrey from Italian models. The Italian or Petrarchan sonnet consists of an octave (rhyming abbaabba) presenting an argument, conflict, or question, followed by a volta (turn of thought) and a sestet (cdecde or cdcdcd) that resolves or meditates on the problem. 

In contrast, the English or Shakespearean sonnet restructured the 14-line iambic pentameter lyric into three quatrains and a concluding rhyming couplet (abab cdcd efef gg). The three quatrains develop three facets of a metaphor, emotion, or philosophical inquiry, while the final heroic couplet provides an epigrammatic twist or summary conclusion. While Petrarch popularized idealized courtly devotion to an unattainable lady (Laura), Shakespeare inverted these conventions in his 154 sonnets by addressing the Fair Youth and the Dark Lady with unsparing psychological realism.
