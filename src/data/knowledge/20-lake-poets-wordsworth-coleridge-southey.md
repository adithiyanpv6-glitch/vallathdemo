---
title: The Lake Poets: Wordsworth, Coleridge, and Southey
author: Wordsworth, Coleridge, Robert Southey
period: Romantic Period
topic: Literary Circles

The 'Lake Poets' is a sobriquet applied derogatorily by Francis Jeffrey in the Edinburgh Review to William Wordsworth, Samuel Taylor Coleridge, and Robert Southey, all of whom lived in the picturesque Lake District of northwestern England around the turn of the nineteenth century. 

United initially by youthful radicalism and enthusiasm for the French Revolution, the Lake Poets pioneered a return to nature, vernacular simplicity, and psychological introspection. While Wordsworth settled at Dove Cottage in Grasmere composing autobiographical epics (The Prelude), Coleridge explored metaphysical philosophy and exotic symbolism (Kubla Khan). Robert Southey, though appointed Poet Laureate in 1813, was subsequently attacked by second-generation Romantics like Lord Byron (in The Vision of Judgment) and Percy Bysshe Shelley for political apostasy and conservative acquiescence.
