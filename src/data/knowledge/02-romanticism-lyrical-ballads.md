---
title: Romanticism & The Lyrical Ballads
author: William Wordsworth & Samuel Taylor Coleridge
period: Romantic Period (1798–1837)
topic: Literary Movements
---

The publication of *Lyrical Ballads* in 1798 by William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge marks the conventional commencement of the English Romantic movement. Reacting against the neoclassical decorum, rigid heroic couplets, and rationalist artificiality of the Augustan Age, Romanticism privileged subjective interiority, intense emotional responsiveness, and organic natural communion. 

In the famous 1800 Preface, Wordsworth defined poetry as 'the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings' recollected in tranquility. He advocated a selection of language really used by men, particularly those in humble, rustic situations where essential passions develop without artificial restraint. While Wordsworth took nature and everyday rustic observation as his domain, Coleridge was tasked with making the supernatural credible through 'that willing suspension of disbelief for the moment, which constitutes poetic faith', as demonstrated in *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner*.
