---
title: Feminist Literary Criticism: Woolf's A Room of One's Own
author: Virginia Woolf
period: Twentieth Century (1929)
topic: Feminist Theory
---

Virginia Woolf's A Room of One's Own (1929), based on lectures delivered at Newnham and Girton Colleges at Cambridge, is a foundational text of feminist literary criticism. Woolf's central thesis is straightforward yet revolutionary: 'a woman must have money and a room of her own if she is to write fiction.' 

Woolf exposed how centuries of legal disenfranchisement, domestic servitude, and economic dependence prevented women from achieving creative genius. To illustrate this historical exclusion, she invented the tragic parable of Judith Shakespeare—William Shakespeare's equally gifted sister—who, denied education and forced into marriage, dies in obscurity without ever writing a line. Woolf also proposed the concept of the 'androgynous mind', arguing that great creative literature transcends narrow gender polarity to unite masculine and feminine faculties.
