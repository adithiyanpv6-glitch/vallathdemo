---
title: W.B. Yeats and Modernist Mythmaking
author: William Butler Yeats
period: Modernist Period / Irish Literary Revival
topic: Modernist Poetry
---

William Butler Yeats, the leading figure of the Irish Literary Revival and winner of the 1923 Nobel Prize in Literature, evolved from Celtic Twilight romanticism into one of the most powerful and demanding voices of twentieth-century Modernism. Confronting Irish political turmoil (the 1916 Easter Rising) and post-war chaos, Yeats developed an intricate esoteric system in A Vision (1925). 

Yeats symbolized historical and psychological cycles through the geometry of interlocking cones or 'gyres'. In his prophetic masterpiece 'The Second Coming' (1920), written in the aftermath of World War I, Yeats announces the terrifying conclusion of a two-thousand-year Christian cycle ('Turning and turning in the widening gyre / The falcon cannot hear the falconer'). Yeats's poetry balances profound political engagement (Easter, 1916) with artistic immortality and spiritual transcendence (Sailing to Byzantium).
