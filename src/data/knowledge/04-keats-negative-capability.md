---
title: Keats's Concept of Negative Capability
author: John Keats
period: Romantic Period
topic: Poetics and Aesthetics
---

John Keats introduced the term 'Negative Capability' in a December 1817 letter to his brothers George and Thomas Keats. He defined it as the quality a man possesses when he is 'capable of being in uncertainties, Mysteries, doubts, without any irritable reaching after fact & reason'. For Keats, the supreme exemplar of this aesthetic virtue was William Shakespeare, who could efface his own ego to inhabit completely different characters—from Iago to Cordelia—without imposing a moralistic verdict. 

Keats contrasted this selfless chameleon poetics with the 'Wordsworthian or egotistical sublime', in which the author's own philosophical persona perpetually dominates the landscape. Negative capability requires the artist to remain open to sensory beauty, contradiction, and psychological ambiguity. In his major 1819 odes (*Ode to a Nightingale*, *Ode on a Grecian Urn*), Keats navigates paradoxical tensions between the timeless perfection of art and the painful mortality of human existence.
