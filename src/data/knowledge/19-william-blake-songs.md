---
title: William Blake: Songs of Innocence and of Experience
author: William Blake
period: Early Romantic Period (1789/1794)
topic: Romantic Poetry & Visionary Art
---

William Blake stands as a revolutionary visionary poet, printmaker, and mystic of the early Romantic period. His masterpiece, Songs of Innocence and of Experience (subtitled 'Shewing the Two Contrary States of the Human Soul'), juxtaposes pairs of lyric poems illustrating contrasting perspectives on pastoral purity and social oppression. 

In Innocence, poems like 'The Lamb' celebrate childhood vulnerability, divine pastoral gentleness, and unfallen spiritual communion. In Experience, corresponding poems like 'The Tyger' confront the terrifying wrath, industrial fire, and predatory energies of existence ('Did he who made the Lamb make thee?'). Through scathing poems like 'London' and 'The Chimney Sweeper', Blake indicted institutional religion, child labour, the monarchy, and the 'mind-forg'd manacles' that imprison human consciousness within capitalist modernity.
