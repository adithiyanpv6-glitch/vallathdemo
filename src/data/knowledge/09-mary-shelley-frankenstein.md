---
title: Mary Shelley's Frankenstein and Romantic Science
author: Mary Shelley
period: Romantic Period (1818)
topic: Gothic and Romantic Novel
---

Mary Wollstonecraft Shelley published *Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus* in 1818, bridging the Gothic romance, Romantic philosophical inquiry, and early science fiction. Written during the rainy summer of 1816 at Villa Diodati near Lake Geneva, the novel reflects contemporary scientific debates regarding galvanism, vitalism, and chemical regeneration alongside Jean-Jacques Rousseau's philosophy of the natural innocence of humanity. 

The epistolary and nested narrative structure (Captain Walton, Victor Frankenstein, the Creature) provides multiple perspectives on overreaching ambition. Victor Frankenstein's hubris in attempting to create life without woman or parental responsibility results in tragic alienation. The Creature's eloquent narration of his education via Milton's *Paradise Lost*, Volney's *Ruins of Empires*, and Plutarch's *Lives* demonstrates that societal rejection and monstrous prejudice, rather than innate villainy, forge human violence.
