---
title: Jane Austen and Free Indirect Discourse
author: Jane Austen
period: Romantic / Regency Era
topic: Novel Technique
---

Jane Austen revolutionized the English novel by transforming domestic manners and courtship into profound studies of psychological self-deception and moral discrimination. Operating within her famous 'little bit (two Inches wide) of Ivory', Austen perfected the narrative technique known as Free Indirect Discourse (style indirect libre). 

Free indirect discourse merges the third-person narrator's voice with the immediate subjective thoughts, vocabulary, and biases of a character without explicit attribution tags like 'she thought' or 'he said'. In *Pride and Prejudice* (1813) and *Emma* (1815), this technique places the reader inside Elizabeth Bennet's or Emma Woodhouse's misjudgments while retaining ironic narrative distance. Through this stylistic breakthrough, Austen exposes how social decorum, patriarchal inheritance laws (entailment), and financial vulnerability govern women's domestic choices.
