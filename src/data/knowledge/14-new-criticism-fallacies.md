---
title: New Criticism: The Intentional and Affective Fallacies
author: W.K. Wimsatt Jr. & Monroe C. Beardsley
period: Mid-Twentieth Century
topic: Literary Theory
---

New Criticism dominated Anglo-American literary scholarship during the mid-twentieth century, advocating 'close reading' and treating the literary text as an autonomous, self-contained aesthetic object ('the verbal icon' or 'well-wrought urn'). Two foundational essays by W.K. Wimsatt and Monroe Beardsley established core New Critical guardrails. 

'The Intentional Fallacy' (1946) argues that judging a poem by the author's biographical intentions or stated purpose is misleading and irrelevant, because a finished poem belongs to the public and must be evaluated solely on its internal textual evidence. Conversely, 'The Affective Fallacy' (1949) warns against judging a literary work by its emotional impact on the reader, which reduces criticism to subjective impressionism. Together with Cleanth Brooks (who emphasized paradox) and I.A. Richards, New Critics focused exclusively on irony, tension, and ambiguity within the text.
