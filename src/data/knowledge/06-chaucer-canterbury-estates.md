---
title: Chaucer's Canterbury Tales & Estates Satire
author: Geoffrey Chaucer
period: Middle English Period (14th Century)
topic: Medieval Literature
---

Geoffrey Chaucer, often hailed as the father of English poetry, composed *The Canterbury Tales* in the late 14th century using the East Midland dialect, which established London English as the standard literary medium. The General Prologue utilizes the medieval genre of Estates Satire, which categorizes society into three estates: the clergy (those who pray), the nobility (those who fight), and the peasantry or labourers (those who work). 

Chaucer gathers twenty-nine pilgrims representing diverse social strata at the Tabard Inn in Southwark traveling to the shrine of Saint Thomas Becket in Canterbury. Through ironic praise and acute sensory details (such as the Monk's love for hunting and the Wife of Bath's gap-toothed vitality), Chaucer exposes ecclesiastical corruption and social mobility following the Black Death. His mastery of the decasyllabic rhyming couplet (the precursor of heroic verse) brought vernacular English poetry to unprecedented sophistication.
