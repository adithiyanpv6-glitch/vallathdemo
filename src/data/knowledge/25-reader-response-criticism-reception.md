---
title: Reader-Response Criticism and Reception Theory
author: Hans Robert Jauss & Wolfgang Iser
period: Late Twentieth Century
topic: Literary Theory
---

Reader-Response criticism emerged in Germany (the Constance School) and the United States during the late 1960s, shifting critical attention from the author or isolated text to the active reading process. Rejecting the New Critical dogma that meaning resides exclusively on the printed page, Reader-Response theorists argued that a literary work only comes to life through the consciousness of the reader. 

Wolfgang Iser examined how literary texts contain 'blanks' or 'gaps' (Leerstellen) that demand imaginative filling and active participation by the implied reader. Meanwhile, Hans Robert Jauss developed Reception Theory (Rezeptionsästhetik), introducing the concept of the 'horizon of expectations'—the set of cultural, historical, and aesthetic assumptions a contemporary audience brings to a text. Stanley Fish extended this to 'interpretive communities', asserting that reading strategies shape textual meaning.
