---
title: Milton and Paradise Lost: Epic Tradition & Theodicy
author: John Milton
period: Civil War and Interregnum / Restoration
topic: Epic Poetry
---

John Milton's *Paradise Lost* (1667) is the definitive English grand epic, composed in unrhymed blank verse ('English heroic verse without rhyme'). Comprising twelve books following Virgil's *Aeneid*, the epic seeks to 'assert Eternal Providence, / And justify the ways of God to men' through the Judeo-Christian narrative of the Fall of humanity. 

The epic is celebrated for its Latinate syntax, cosmic scale, invocation of the Uranian Muse, and complex characterization of Satan. In Books I and II, Satan embodies heroic tragic grandeur, defiance, and rhetorical sublimity, famously declaring that the mind 'can make a Heaven of Hell, a Hell of Heaven'. However, as the narrative progresses to Books IV and IX, Satan degenerates from an archangel into a degraded spy and literal serpent. William Blake famously observed in *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell* that Milton was 'of the Devil\'s party without knowing it', highlighting the poet's subversive imaginative power.
