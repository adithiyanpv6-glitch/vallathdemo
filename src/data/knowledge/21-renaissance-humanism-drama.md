---
title: Renaissance Humanism and Elizabethan Drama
author: Christopher Marlowe & William Shakespeare
period: Renaissance / Elizabethan Era
topic: Early Modern Drama
---

The Elizabethan era witnessed an explosive fusion of Renaissance Humanism, classical Roman tragedy (Seneca), and native medieval morality play traditions. Humanism revived classical scholarship, championing human agency, secular inquiry, and linguistic eloquence over medieval scholastic dogmatism. 

Christopher Marlowe established blank verse as the majestic medium of English drama with his 'mighty line'. Marlowe's overreaching protagonists—such as Tamburlaine with his lust for military conquest, and Doctor Faustus with his thirst for forbidden intellectual and necromantic power—exemplified the limitless ambitions and perilous pride of the Renaissance individual. William Shakespeare built upon Marlowe's structural and metrical foundations, expanding Elizabethan drama into universal explorations of political power (Macbeth), epistemological doubt (Hamlet), and cosmic filial betrayal (King Lear).
