---
title: Samuel Beckett and the Theatre of the Absurd
author: Samuel Beckett
period: Post-War Modernism / Absurdism
topic: Drama
---

Coined by critic Martin Esslin in 1961, the 'Theatre of the Absurd' describes post-World War II dramatists who expressed Albert Camus's philosophical premise that human existence is fundamentally devoid of transcendental purpose, metaphysical certainty, or coherent communication. 

Samuel Beckett's Waiting for Godot (1953) is the archetypal absurd drama. On a barren country road beside a solitary tree, two tramps, Vladimir and Estragon, wait endlessly for the mysterious Godot, who never arrives. The play rejects conventional Aristotelian dramatic progression (exposition, climax, resolution), replacing linear plot with circular dialogue, vaudevillian slapstick, and repetitive pauses. As critic Vivian Mercier famously quipped, Beckett created a play where 'nothing happens, twice'. Through tragicomic paralysis, Beckett dramatizes the existential endurance of humanity in a silent, godless universe.
