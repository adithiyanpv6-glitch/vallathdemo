---
title: T.S. Eliot and the 'Objective Correlative'
author: T.S. Eliot
period: Modernist Period (20th Century)
topic: Literary Theory and Poetics
---

In his seminal 1919 critical essay 'Hamlet and His Problems', T.S. Eliot introduced the foundational concept of the 'objective correlative'. Eliot asserted: 'The only way of expressing emotion in the form of art is by finding an objective correlative; in other words, a set of objects, a situation, a chain of events which shall be the formula of that particular emotion; such that when the external facts, which must terminate in sensory experience, are given, the emotion is immediately evoked.' 

Eliot claimed Shakespeare's *Hamlet* was an artistic failure because Hamlet's disgust was buffeted by an emotion in excess of the facts as presented (his mother's guilt was inadequate to justify his neurosis). In his landmark poem *The Waste Land* (1922), Eliot practiced this theory by assembling mythic fragments, dry bones, urban decay, and multilingual allusions as objective correlatives for the spiritual bankruptcy and psychological fragmentation of post-World War I European civilization.
