---
title: Virginia Woolf and Stream of Consciousness
author: Virginia Woolf
period: Modernist Period
topic: Modernist Fiction
---

Virginia Woolf was a central figure of the Bloomsbury Group who rebelled against the materialist realism of Edwardian novelists like Arnold Bennett and John Galsworthy. In her manifesto essay 'Modern Fiction' (1919), she urged writers to 'examine for an instant an ordinary mind on an ordinary day... Let us record the atoms as they fall upon the mind in the order in which they fall.' 

To capture this luminous halo of inner reality, Woolf pioneered the stream-of-consciousness technique, rendering interior monologue, fleeting memories, and psychological associations. In *Mrs Dalloway* (1925), a single June day in London alternates between Clarissa Dalloway's preparations for an evening party and Septimus Warren Smith's shell-shocked trauma from World War I. Through the striking tolling of Big Ben and rhythmic transitions between character consciousnesses, Woolf reveals how time, mortality, and sensory experience intertwine.
