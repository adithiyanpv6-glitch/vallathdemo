---
title: The Gothic Tradition in English Fiction
author: Horace Walpole, Ann Radcliffe, Matthew Lewis
period: Eighteenth Century to Victorian
topic: Gothic Fiction
---

The Gothic novel originated with Horace Walpole's The Castle of Otranto (1764) and flourished throughout the late eighteenth century as an imaginative counter-current to Enlightenment rationalism. Gothic fiction is characterized by medieval castles, subterranean vaults, ancestral curses, tyrant villains, persecuted maidens, and the supernatural. 

Ann Radcliffe perfected the 'explained supernatural' or female Gothic in The Mysteries of Udolpho (1794), where uncanny terrors are ultimately demystified by rational explanations. Conversely, Matthew Gregory Lewis embraced sensational horror, transgressive eroticism, and genuine demonic intervention in The Monk (1796). The Gothic tradition deeply influenced the Brontë sisters (Emily Brontë's Wuthering Heights), Charles Dickens, and Bram Stoker's Dracula, embodying societal anxieties regarding psychological repression and ancestral retribution.
