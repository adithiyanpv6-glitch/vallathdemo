---
title: Postcolonial Theory: Edward Said's Orientalism
author: Edward W. Said
period: Contemporary / Postcolonial (1978)
topic: Postcolonial Studies
---

Edward Said's Orientalism (1978) inaugurated modern Postcolonial Studies by demonstrating how European scholarship, literature, and imperial politics constructed an imaginary, monolithic 'Orient'. Drawing upon Michel Foucault's concept of discourse and Antonio Gramsci's theory of cultural hegemony, Said argued that Orientalism is not merely a collection of falsehoods, but a systematic Western discourse through which Europe produced, managed, and subordinated the East. 

Said showed that Western texts persistently constructed binary oppositions: the Occident was rational, progressive, virtuous, and masculine, whereas the Orient was exoticized as passive, irrational, despotic, and decadent. This epistemic framework legitimized direct colonial conquest under the guise of the 'civilizing mission'. Said's work stimulated subsequent colonial discourse analysis by Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak (Can the Subaltern Speak?) and Homi Bhabha (theories of mimicry, hybridity, and ambivalence).
